About

The research background,
the strategy orientation

My path has not been linear. I started in marketing intelligence and child development research, spent years at the PhD level in quantitative methods and psychology, and moved through applied research and strategy roles. Most of that work had a common thread: figuring out how to measure things that resist easy measurement, and building systems honest about what they do not know.

Most of my work happens where qualitative and quantitative research meet a product decision. I've built segmentation surveys on factor analysis and clustering, and I've sat through usability sessions where one person's hesitation told me more than the survey did. Most of the job is knowing which one a question needs. Lately a lot of those questions are about AI, but I'm not interested in output that only looks impressive. What I want to know is whether the system actually improves the decisions users are trying to make, for which users, under what conditions, and how you would know if those decisions started getting worse again.

I am skeptical of demos. I am interested in what happens six months after launch. I think trust is a measurement problem and that most AI products are solving the wrong version of it.

I've worked at Fisher-Price, on DoD programs (Secret clearance, now inactive), and at Crowley Webb/Praxis, where clients included AAA, AbbVie, and M&T Bank. At Praxis I built a consumer and clinical trial recruitment research panel from zero to ~1,000 participants, an infrastructure project that shaped how I think about research operations and the conditions that make good research possible at scale. The scale of the work changes; the discipline does not.
