About

Still asking the same question

I’ve wanted to know how things work for as long as I can remember. Mostly that meant taking them apart, and my dad still hasn’t fully forgiven me for the pool filter. It also meant sorting the world into categories, from backyard insects to wrapping paper patterns. The question that fascinated me longest, though, was about people. Why did adults say one thing and then do something else? Just resting their eyes while snoring loud enough to rattle the windows, or promising they knew a shortcut right before making three U-turns in a row.

I’ve been chasing some version of that ever since. After starting in marketing intelligence, I moved into product research at Fisher-Price, where the end users were kids. Then I earned a PhD in psychology and quantitative methods and went on to lead research for agency and consulting clients, including pharma and healthtech companies, a bank, and the Department of Defense. Behavioral data shows what people do, and talking with them shows what they say. I still go looking for the places where those don’t match.

These days a lot of my questions are about when people trust what AI tells them, when they check, and what happens to that trust the first time it’s wrong.
