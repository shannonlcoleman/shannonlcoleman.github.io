Research Panel  •  Crowley Webb / Praxis  •  2020–2022

Building a Research Panel Regulated Clients Could Trust

A proprietary research panel of ~1,000 opted-in participants, built from zero for an agency whose pharma and banking clients answer to regulators

TL;DR

What changed
Studies drew from the agency’s own panel instead of third-party vendors.
Result
External recruitment spend went to $0, and each study’s recruitment took 2+ weeks less.
My role
Researcher and panel architect. I designed the panel solo and built it primarily on my own.
Timeline
About a year
Why it mattered
The agency needed to win and keep regulated pharma and banking clients, and to run studies faster and for less.
Read the full case study
What I built

Three pieces, built to hold up to scrutiny

An opt-in at the end of every study

Built in Qualtrics. People who finished a study got a clear invitation to join the panel, kept separate from the study itself. Joining was never a condition of finishing.

A referral program with traceable codes

Each member got a unique code, and attribution logic I designed tied every new participant to their source. Rewards ran through Amazon at first, then Giftbit.

Compliance built in from the start, with legal

It shaped every data handling decision from the first plan on. Participant data lived in Azure SQL.

The decision

Buy a list, or build from opt-ins

The shortcut

Buy email lists.

What it meant

Volume sooner. A purchased list doesn’t show where anyone came from or whether they agreed to be contacted.

What I chose

Build it from people who chose to join.

What it meant

Slower to start, with proof of where every participant came from.

My manager was adamant from the start that we would buy email lists and had a hard time letting that go. I knew during the research phase I wouldn’t, and I raised it once the intake survey was built and we were close to fielding, so I had a working alternative to show. My manager came around.

The challenge

Regulated clients and no panel of our own

Pharma and banking clients answer to regulators, so the agency needed participants it could vouch for.

Crowley Webb is an advertising agency that also owns Praxis, a clinical trial recruitment firm, and I worked for both. Praxis brought the pharma relationships, and the agency had banking clients too. Every study that needed screened participants went to a third-party vendor, which meant paying vendor rates on top of the research and accepting whatever panel quality they offered.

Approach

Research and compliance came first

Research, planning, approval, and compliance took almost half the year before anyone could join, which is why growth started slow.

Reflection

Impact

Building the panel from scratch convinced me that consent and compliance have to be designed in from the start, because they can’t be added later. For the agency, studies could move at the pace pharma and banking clients expected, with participants we could vouch for.

Hindsight

1
Turn my process into a playbook sooner

I documented as I went, but around my own workflow. A playbook someone else could run would have made the panel easier to hand off and extend without me.

2
Plan the participant lifecycle up front

Recruitment got the attention because that’s where the pressure was. Re-engagement for inactive members and a refresh plan were handled as they came up. Designing them in from the start would have cut attrition and kept the panel’s makeup cleaner.

Next steps

With two more weeks

1
Compare quality by source

Check response rates, completion, and screener pass rates for opt-in members against referrals.

2
Test the panel against a vendor

Run the same screener on both and compare quality and cost directly.

3
Put a number on the savings

Turn $0 in external recruitment spend into a dollar figure based on past vendor rates.
