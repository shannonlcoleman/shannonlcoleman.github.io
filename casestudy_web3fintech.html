When “Pending” Looked Like Lost Money

A UX and regulatory audit of a platform for investing in real-world assets through blockchain-based tokens, before launch.

TL;DR

What changed
The client relabeled its transaction states, added a progress bar and an “In settlement” balance, and moved the product explainer ahead of wallet setup. UX and compliance also became one workstream, and compliance approval went from 6–8 weeks to 3–5.
Projected impact
The roadmap projected a 22% lift in 90-day retention and about half as many support tickets.
My role
Research lead and strategic advisor, from the audit through the executive roadmap and implementation support
Timeline
6 weeks: 1 of discovery, 2 of research, 1 of synthesis, 2 of readout and implementation support
Why it mattered
Users who think their money is gone don’t wait around. They call support or leave, right when a new financial product most needs their trust.
Read the full case study
The situation

Traditional finance on the surface, blockchain underneath

The client let people invest in real-world assets through blockchain-based tokens, inside a regulated framework. The interface looked like a familiar banking app. Underneath, settlement happened in stages over minutes to hours, and the screen didn’t show those stages.

I was brought in before launch to audit onboarding and the full transaction flow, find the friction most likely to hurt retention, and deliver a roadmap ranked by both UX and regulatory risk.

Approach

Comparing what the screen said with what people understood

The interface worked. Buttons did what they should, and nothing was visually broken. So rather than run a standard usability review, I looked at the gap between what each screen communicated and what someone used to a bank app would take from it:

  • A walkthrough of every state from first deposit through withdrawal
  • Mapping the expectations people brought from traditional finance
  • A step-by-step review of onboarding order and disclosures
  • A review of the regulatory language against what users actually understood
Findings

11 of 17 friction points came from one place

The audit found 17 friction points. Eleven were about transaction states: what the screen showed while money was moving. Every state was technically accurate, and people still read a normal settlement delay as lost money.

Recommendations

Fix the product and the disclosures together

The gaps that made “pending” feel like lost money were the same gaps that created regulatory risk when people misunderstood their positions. So the roadmap ranked all 17 findings by combined UX and regulatory risk, with owners in product, content, and compliance, instead of splitting them into separate workstreams.

What I’d do differently

Two changes in hindsight

1
Run the onboarding and transaction reviews at the same time

I started with transactions because that’s where the retention risk pointed. But people who skipped the explainer during onboarding were the ones most likely to panic at a pending state. Running both reviews together would have connected those findings sooner.

2
Test the disclosure language directly

The regulatory findings mattered more than their count of two suggests. Comprehension testing on the disclosure language would have given that recommendation stronger evidence. Time and budget didn’t allow it, and it’s the piece I’d add first.
